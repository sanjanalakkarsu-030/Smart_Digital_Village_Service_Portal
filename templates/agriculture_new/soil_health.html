🌿 1. Soil Testing for Better Crop Yield

Soil testing helps farmers understand their field's condition and make informed decisions about fertilizers and soil management.

🔬

Why Is Soil Testing Important?

  • Helps identify soil pH and nutrient status.
  • Supports selection of suitable fertilizers.
  • Helps avoid unnecessary fertilizer expenses.
  • Supports balanced nutrient management.
  • Helps farmers plan crop nutrition based on soil condition.

Farmer Benefit

Soil testing can support better crop management and yield potential. Actual yield also depends on weather, irrigation, crop variety, pests and other factors.

🧪

How to Collect a Soil Sample

  1. Choose representative spots in your field.
  2. Avoid unusual areas such as fertilizer piles, field bunds and drainage spots.
  3. Use clean tools and a clean container.
  4. Collect and mix soil as instructed by the testing lab.
  5. Label the sample with the required field details.
  6. Submit it to a soil testing laboratory as instructed.

Tip: Ask your local Agriculture Officer about the correct sampling depth and quantity for your field.

🌾 2. Crop-wise Fertilizer Guidance

Select your crop to understand the basic principles of nutrient management. Exact fertilizer quantities should follow the soil test report and verified local recommendations.

🌾
CROP 01

Paddy (Rice)

Balanced nutrient management helps support healthy rice growth, tillering and grain development.

  • Nitrogen (N): Supports leaf and plant growth.
  • Phosphorus (P): Supports root development.
  • Potassium (K): Supports plant functions and crop health.

Good practice: Follow the recommended fertilizer schedule for your rice variety, soil test and crop stage. Apply nitrogen in recommended splits where advised.

🌱
CROP 02

Cotton

Cotton needs balanced nutrition throughout its growth. Requirements vary according to soil, variety and field conditions.

  • Nitrogen (N): Supports vegetative growth.
  • Phosphorus (P): Supports root development.
  • Potassium (K): Supports plant development and crop quality.

Good practice: Base fertilizer choices on soil test results and local cotton recommendations. Avoid excessive nitrogen without expert advice.

⚠️ Important Fertilizer Safety Note

These are general nutrient explanations, not a fertilizer prescription. Exact doses and application timing are not provided here because they depend on local recommendations, soil test results, crop variety and growing conditions. Consult your Agriculture Officer before deciding quantities.

🍃 3. Organic Farming & Natural Soil Care

Organic soil management practices can help maintain soil organic matter, improve soil structure and support long-term soil health when used appropriately.

♻️

Farmyard Manure & Compost

  • Use well-decomposed farmyard manure or compost.
  • Organic matter can help improve soil structure.
  • Compost contributes organic matter and nutrients.
  • Use mature compost and follow local recommendations.
🌿

Green Manure & Crop Residues

  • Green manure crops can add organic matter to soil.
  • Manage crop residues appropriately instead of burning them.
  • Organic matter can support soil moisture retention.
  • Choose practices suitable for your crop and field.

🌱 Organic Farming Tip

Organic inputs improve soil management, but their nutrient content varies. They may not provide every nutrient in the quantity a crop requires. Combine soil testing with locally recommended nutrient management for informed decisions.
